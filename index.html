Cognitive Readiness Institute

Est. 2026

Open research on human judgement

Cognitive readiness is the capacity to keep thinking well as the demands on thinking rise.

The Institute exists to establish what the new information dynamics around us are doing to our minds, and what can be done about it — published openly, including the findings that are inconvenient.

01 What is happening

Capability is falling, and it started before AI.

More information reaches each of us than ever. It arrives increasingly designed to persuade. And a growing share of the thinking is now available to be handed over. Our capacity to absorb it has not moved since 1980.

  • The reversal predates the assistant

    US long-term-trend mathematics scores peaked in 2012 — the year smartphone ownership crossed half the adult population — and are back at 1990s levels.

  • It is environmental, not genetic

    The decline replicates within families across 730,000 Norwegian conscripts. Brothers raised later score lower than brothers raised earlier. Whatever is causing it is in the conditions, not the gene pool.

  • AI steepens the curve

    Across separate controlled trials the same shape appears: performance rises while the assistant is available, then lands below that of people who never used one. It has been measured in experts — nineteen endoscopists with thousands of procedures each detected measurably less unaided, once AI assistance had become routine.

Less cognitive readiness makes engaging cognition harder, which makes the tool feel more necessary, which means less practice.

03 The framework

Readiness reads in four layers.

Each layer sets the ceiling for the one above it — and all of it runs inside conditions somebody else designed. The ten capacities are assembled from the published literature. The layering, the pipeline reading and the conditions band are the Institute's own, and are stated as claims rather than findings.

04 Goals

What the Institute exists to do.

  • —

    Collect and assemble the research

    Twenty-five years of accumulating work on attention, reading, reasoning and offloading sits scattered across neuroscience, behavioural decision science, human factors and occupational psychology. The Institute gathers it into one framework.

  • —

    Run the annual Cognitive Readiness Survey

    Free and open to any organisation, building a public benchmark for how cognitive readiness is actually distributed — rather than how people report it.

  • —

    Publish openly

    Framework, method and findings in public, free to read and free to use.

  • —

    Raise awareness

    Establish what the new information dynamics around us are doing to human judgement, and what can be done about it.

The work observes behaviour rather than asking for self-report. On attention, self-assessment tracks measured ability at approximately r = .08, while some seven in ten people rate themselves above average — and observer judgement is no safer.
